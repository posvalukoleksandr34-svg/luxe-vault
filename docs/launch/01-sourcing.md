# 1. Sourcing & Supplier Strategy

## 1.1 The four sourcing tiers

Ranked by what actually fits a 500 CHF budget and a "Dark Luxury" promise.
Tier 1 is where you start. Tiers 3–4 are the upgrade path once cash allows.

### Tier 1 — Private sourcing agent in China ← **start here**

This is the only tier that works with no inventory capital. An agent buys from
the domestic Chinese wholesale market on your behalf, quality-checks, rebrands,
and ships one parcel per order to your customer.

**Where the product actually is:**
- **1688.com** — the domestic wholesale platform. This is the real source
  behind most of AliExpress and a large share of Alibaba. Prices are 2–4×
  lower. You cannot buy directly without a Chinese entity and payment method —
  hence the agent. Search in Chinese: `光面羽绒服` (glossy down jacket),
  `面包服` (puffer / "bread jacket"), `重磅卫衣` (heavyweight hoodie),
  `美式高街` (American high-street), `暗黑风` (dark style).
- **Taobao / Tmall** — higher-end designer-ish Chinese labels, better finishing,
  2–3× the 1688 price. Worth it for hero pieces.
- **Guangzhou Shisanhang / Shahe** and **Hangzhou Sijiqing** markets — where a
  good agent physically walks the stalls for you.

**How to find a real agent (not a reseller in disguise):**
1. Referrals first. Ask in operator communities, not public groups: Discord and
   Telegram sourcing servers, the private Slack/Discord of any ecom course,
   LinkedIn outreach to EU-based brand owners who already ship from China.
2. Public routes, in descending quality: agent directories, Facebook groups
   ("Dropshipping Agents", "Ecom Sourcing Agents"), X/Twitter ecom circles.
3. Platform agents as a fallback only: **CJdropshipping**, **Zendrop**,
   **Eprolo**, **Sup Dropshipping**. Fast to set up, but their catalogue ceiling
   is generic goods — you will not find Dark Luxury there. Use them to test
   logistics, not to build a brand.

**The five questions that disqualify 80% of agents in one message:**
1. "Send me your WeChat, your warehouse address, and a 30-second video walking
    through it today." (Refusal or stock footage → out.)
2. "Which line do you use to Switzerland specifically, and what is your
    measured door-to-door time for the last 20 parcels?" (Vague → out.
    Switzerland is not the EU and many lines quote EU times.)
3. "Do you ship **DDP** to Switzerland and the EU — duties and VAT prepaid, so
    my customer is never invoiced at the door?" (No → out. Non-negotiable.)
4. "Can you do custom woven labels, custom hangtags, and a branded poly mailer
    or box, and at what MOQ?" (No → out. It's a brand, not a shop.)
5. "Who pays for a unit the customer rejects as defective?" (Get it in writing.)

**What a good agent costs:** nothing upfront. They take a margin on the product
price or a flat 1–3 USD per order handling fee. Pay by Wise/PayPal for the
first orders — never bank transfer to a personal account until trust is built.

### Tier 2 — Alibaba, verified manufacturers (private label, Phase 2)

Use when you have ~1,500–3,000 CHF and want your own cut, fabric and labels.

- Filter hard: **Verified Supplier** + **Trade Assurance** + 5+ years +
  response rate >90%. Ignore everything else.
- Outerwear clusters: **Zhejiang** (Pinghu is the down-jacket capital),
  **Jiangsu**, **Guangdong** (streetwear, heavyweight jersey).
- Typical MOQ 50–100 pcs per colourway. Ask for a **sample order** first —
  most will do 1–3 pcs at 2–5× unit price. Always pay it.
- Demand documents before you pay: **OEKO-TEX Standard 100** (fabric safety),
  **BSCI or Sedex/SMETA** audit (social compliance — an EU retailer will
  eventually be asked), and for real down, **RDS** (Responsible Down Standard).

### Tier 3 — Turkey (Istanbul) ← **best quality-per-franc for Europe**

The sweet spot most Swiss/EU brands end up at. Quality between China and Italy,
prices 30–60% above China, MOQ 10–50 per style, and **3–6 days by road to
Switzerland** — which solves the delivery problem outright.

- Districts: **Merter** and **Laleli** (wholesale), **Osmanbey** (design-led).
- Route in: fly to Istanbul for two days and walk it — it is a ~250 CHF flight
  and it is worth more than three months of emails. Failing that, use a Turkish
  sourcing agent (they are cheap and plentiful) or Turkish B2B marketplaces.
- Turkey has a **customs union with the EU** for industrial goods — EU-bound
  shipments move duty-free with an A.TR certificate. Switzerland has its own
  FTA with Turkey. Ask every supplier for the A.TR / EUR.1 paperwork.

### Tier 4 — Italy / Portugal (true luxury, Phase 3)

What you use once the brand can carry a 249–399 CHF price. Not now.

- **Italy:** Veneto and Marche for outerwear, Carpi for knitwear, Prato for
  fabrics. Find them at **Pitti Uomo** (Florence, Jan/Jun), **Milano Unica**
  (fabrics), **White Milano**. MOQ 30–100, unit costs 60–150 EUR.
- **Portugal:** Porto / Braga / Guimarães. Far more willing on small MOQs
  (30–50) and roughly 60% of Italian cost. The best value in EU manufacturing
  for jersey, hoodies and heavyweight cotton.
- **Matchmaking platforms** that save you months: **Sqetch**, **MakersValley**,
  **Foursource**, **Common Objective**.

### Tier 5 — European wholesalers (a bridge, not a strategy)

- **BrandsGateway / BrandsDistribution / Griffati** — genuine designer stock,
  1–3 day EU delivery, dropship-friendly. But you resell *other people's*
  brands at 20–40% margin. It builds no equity in Luxe Vault. Skip.
- **Faire** and **Ankorstore** — wholesale from independent European brands.
  Worth a real look on a 500 CHF budget: both give new retailers opening
  credit and **net-60 payment terms**, so you can stock 2–3 genuinely premium
  European pieces *without paying upfront*, ship them from your own address in
  2–4 days, and use them as the credibility anchors of the first collection
  while your own-label production spins up. This is the single best cash-flow
  hack available to you this month.

## 1.2 Verifying "Dark Luxury" quality without visiting a factory

Seven checks, in order. Numbers 1 and 2 are mandatory; skipping them is how
brands die on their first 20 refunds.

**1. Buy samples. Always. From 2–3 suppliers, same style, same week.**
Budget ~150 CHF of your 500 for this. Ship them express (DHL/FedEx, 4–7 days)
to your own address, not the cheap line. You are buying information, not stock.

**2. Send a tech pack / spec sheet *before* quoting, and make them confirm it
in writing.** For a glossy puffer, the spec that separates 20 CHF from 60 CHF:

| Spec | What to demand | Cheap tell |
|---|---|---|
| Shell fabric | Matte-glossy nylon, 40–75 GSM, **downproof coating** | Crinkly, plasticky sheen; visible weave |
| Fill | 90/10 white duck down, 600+ fill power, or high-loft poly wadding with stated GSM | "Cotton filling" (= polyester scrap), flat panels |
| Fill weight | Stated in grams per size (e.g. 180 g for M) | Supplier cannot state it |
| Baffles | Even, no puckering, stitch-through or box-baffle stated | Wavy stitch lines, down leakage |
| Zips | **YKK** (main + pockets), branded pull | Unbranded, sticky, light action |
| Lining | Full lining, bound seams | Raw overlocked seams |
| Stitch density | 8–12 SPI | Loose, skipped stitches |
| Labels | Custom woven neck label + care label in EN/DE/FR/IT | Printed transfer label |

**3. Demand a video of *your actual unit*, not stock photography.** 60 seconds,
daylight, hands on the garment: zip pulled up and down, fabric flexed, label
close-up, seams inside-out. Any agent who will not do this is wrong for you.

**4. Photograph the sample under your own studio lighting before you commit.**
This is the specific Dark Luxury killer: glossy fabrics that look expensive in
a showroom look like a bin bag under a flash. Shoot it against your site's own
background. If it does not look like the brand, the product is not the brand.

**5. Wear it for a week.** Wash it once. Check: does the sheen dull, do the
baffles migrate, does the zip still run, does the black fade to grey. Black
that goes grey after one wash is the most common failure in cheap outerwear
and it generates refunds two months after the sale.

**6. Third-party pre-shipment inspection, from your first 50-unit order.**
**QIMA**, **SGS**, **Bureau Veritas**, **TÜV SÜD**, **V-Trust**. Roughly
USD 100–300 for an AQL 2.5 sampling inspection at the factory before the goods
ship. Cheap insurance against a whole batch being wrong.

**7. Compliance paperwork that EU/CH selling actually requires.** Get these on
file now, not when someone asks: fibre composition per EU Regulation 1007/2011
(a textile label stating exact fibre percentages is legally required in the
EU), care symbols, country of origin, and the OEKO-TEX certificate.

## 1.3 Logistics: guaranteeing 10–14 business days

Your site promises **10–14 business days = 14–20 calendar days**
(`config/shipping.ts`, rendered through `businessToCalendarDays()` in
`lib/fulfilment.ts`). That window is achievable, but only on a specific model.

### The three models, honestly compared

| Model | Door-to-door | Cost | Capital needed | Hits 14–20 days? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Agent dropship, China → CH/EU direct, premium line** | 8–15 business days | 8–16 USD/parcel | **None** | Yes, with ~4 days of margin |
| **B. Agent dropship, cheap line (China Post / e-packet)** | 15–35 business days | 3–6 USD | None | **No — do not use** |
| **C. Bulk to EU/CH 3PL, local last mile** | 1–4 business days | ~3–6 CHF last mile + storage | 1,500 CHF+ | Yes, easily |
| **D. Turkey / Portugal direct** | 3–7 business days | 12–25 EUR | Medium (MOQ) | Yes |

### The answer: A now, C from ~order 50

**Phase 1 — Model A (weeks 1–6, this budget).**
Agent dropship on a **premium/special line only**. Named lines that actually
hold the window to Switzerland and the EU:
- **YunExpress** (Priority / Special Line, DDP variants)
- **SFC Service** (strong on EU DDP)
- **4PX** (Special Line to EU)
- **Cainiao Premium**
- For the first orders, **DHL eCommerce** or **DHL Express** if the agent
  offers it — expensive (18–30 USD) but 4–7 days, and worth eating the cost on
  your first ten customers to buy reviews and photos.

Operating rules that make the promise hold:
- **Dispatch within 48 h of payment.** Your window has ~4 days of slack; burn
  it on the supplier side and you miss.
- **Ship DDP, always.** See § 1.4.
- **Tracking number into the shipped email within 72 h.** The repo already
  carries tracking in the shipped email (`lib/server/emails/lifecycle.ts`) and
  courier links via `courierTrackingUrl()` — use them.
- **Under-promise per product.** Each product carries its own
  `deliveryDays` (admin panel, migration 0026). Set slower lines individually
  rather than moving the store-wide window.

**Phase 2 — Model C (from ~50 orders / ~1,500 CHF free cash).**
Bulk-air your best two SKUs to a European 3PL and you go from 14–20 days to
2–4 days, which roughly doubles conversion on its own.

Choosing the hub is a customs question, not a price question — **Switzerland is
outside the EU customs union and outside EU VAT**:
- **Majority of sales Swiss** → a Swiss 3PL. **Swiss Post Fulfilment**,
  **Planzer**, **Galliker**, **Bächli/It's-Logistics**-class providers. Domestic
  next-day, no customs per order. EU orders then export from CH (needs DDP).
- **Majority of sales EU** → a Dutch/German 3PL. **Monta** (NL), **byrd**
  (AT/DE — good CH coverage), **ShipBob EU** (NL), **Huboo**, **Salesupply**,
  **Bergen Logistics**. One customs clearance on the inbound bulk shipment, then
  free circulation across the EU.
- **Both** → start with the one matching >60% of your orders; split later.
  Do not split stock at your volume.

### 1.4 Customs & VAT — the thing that silently kills premium brands

If your customer is asked to pay anything at the door, the premium experience
is over and you get a refund request. Two jurisdictions, two fixes:

**Switzerland.** Import VAT is 8.1% on clothing. The de-minimis rule waives it
only when the VAT owed is ≤ CHF 5 — i.e. goods + shipping below ~CHF 62. A
109 CHF jacket is **well over** that, so every Swiss parcel attracts ~9 CHF VAT
*plus* a carrier handling fee (Swiss Post charges roughly CHF 11.50–16). That
is a ~25 CHF surprise invoice on a 134 CHF order.
→ **Fix: ship DDP.** Your agent prepays. Build it into landed cost.
→ Swiss VAT registration (MWST) becomes mandatory at **CHF 100,000** worldwide
turnover. The mail-order rule (Versandhandelsregelung) also pulls foreign
sellers in at CHF 100k of small-consignment sales into CH.

**European Union.** Since 2021 there is no VAT exemption on low-value imports.
For consignments up to **EUR 150**, register for **IOSS** — you charge EU VAT
at checkout and the parcel clears without the customer being touched. At a
109 CHF price point you are comfortably inside the IOSS threshold.
→ As a non-EU (Swiss) seller you need an **IOSS intermediary**: **Eurora**,
**Ship24**, **Crossborderit**, **Taxdoo**, **Hellotax**. Roughly EUR 30–100/month.
→ Without IOSS, your EU customers get billed VAT + a 10–15 EUR clearance fee at
the door. Do not launch EU ads before this is solved.

**Returns.** A Chinese return address on a premium brand is fatal — and EU
consumers have a statutory **14-day right of withdrawal** (Distance Selling
Directive) that you must honour regardless. Practical approach at this volume:
publish a Swiss returns address (your own, or a virtual address ~20 CHF/month),
and for low-value defects issue a refund without demanding the item back — the
return freight usually exceeds the landed cost.
